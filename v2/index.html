How you move through the app

01

Open to Today, navigate by thumb

The app opens on Today — the date, one big “Take attendance,” and who’s expected. A bottom bar (Today · Roster · Eligibility · More) keeps everything one thumb-tap away instead of reaching for tabs up top.

Why it matters: you open the phone right after class, standing up. The first thing you see should be today’s roll, not a spreadsheet.

Taking the roll on a phone

02

Tap a name to mark present

A clean list of every student. Tap once → present (turns red). The count climbs live at the top. One Save writes the whole class. No pinch-zooming a wide grid.

Why it matters: this is the daily job. Big tap targets beat tiny spreadsheet cells, and it pairs with the paste-from-WhatsApp flow you already have.

03

Swipe through a big class

One student at a time, full screen. Flick right = present, left = absent. Made for the 18–20 person Sunday classes where you want rhythm, not scrolling.

Why it matters: an optional “fast mode” next to the tap list — some senseis will love it, some won’t. You’d pick whichever feels right in the dojo.

The point: who’s ready to test

04

A readiness card per student

Tap any student to see attendance %, current streak, last seen — and the five exam qualifiers as a checklist: attendance, active training, simulations, seminars, and willingness to travel to New Orleans.

Why it matters: attendance is the gateway, but the real question Vazrik asks is “is this student ready?” This turns the app from a log into the qualification tool it’s meant to be.

The whole class, ranked by readiness

Keeping students showing up

06

Streaks, comebacks & milestones

Each student earns marks for showing up: longest streak, a perfect month, a comeback after missing. A monthly podium for the top three.

Why it matters: consistent attendance is literally a black-belt qualifier. A little recognition turns “taking attendance” into something that pulls students back to the mat.

The visual identity

07

A real identity, not blue-on-white

This whole page is the proposal: sumi-ink black, gi-canvas cream, a single hinomaru red, and gold reserved for “earned.” Two modes — a dark Dojo and a light Gi — you’d pick the default.

Why it matters: the app feeds something serious — black-belt eligibility. It should look like the JKA, not a generic spreadsheet.

Dojo · dark
For the dojo at night — ink, indigo, hinomaru red, belt gold.
Gi · light
Bright, canvas-white, easy to read in daylight.
